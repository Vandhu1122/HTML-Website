1. Flex Direction (The Main Axis)

flex-direction establishes the main axis along which flex items are placed in the container:

row (Default)

Items flow horizontally from left to right (in standard LTR writing modes).

column

Items stack vertically from top to bottom, making the vertical axis the main axis.

row-reverse

Items flow horizontally in reverse order from right to left.

column-reverse

Items stack vertically from bottom to top in reverse order.

2. Justify Content (Main Axis Distribution)

justify-content defines how remaining space along the main axis is distributed between and around items:

flex-start

Items are packed toward the start line of the container.

center

Items are grouped together at the visual center of the main axis.

flex-end

Items are packed toward the end line of the container.

space-between

First and last items touch outer edges; remaining items are spaced evenly between.

space-around

Items share equal space on both sides (outer gaps are half the inner gaps).

space-evenly

Equal spacing is distributed between every item and the boundary walls.

3. Align Items (Cross Axis Alignment)

align-items controls how flex items align along the perpendicular cross axis:

stretch (Default)

Items stretch to fill the height (or width in column mode) of the container unless given fixed dimensions.

center

Items are centered along the cross axis line.

flex-start

Items align flush to the cross-start boundary.

flex-end

Items align flush to the cross-end boundary.

4. Flex Wrap & Spacing (gap)

By default, flex containers attempt to squeeze all items onto one line (flex-wrap: nowrap). Setting flex-wrap: wrap enables elements to break naturally onto new lines as viewport space constrains them. Modern browsers also support gap, which eliminates the need for negative margin hacks between items.
